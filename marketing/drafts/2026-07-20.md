## LinkedIn — cost-visibility post (longer form)

Most teams adopting AI coding agents have no idea what they cost, and the reason is structural rather than careless.

Traditional LLM observability — Langfuse, LangSmith, and the rest — works by instrumenting your code. You import an SDK, wrap your calls, and traces flow to a dashboard. That's a good fit when you own the application.

But nobody owns Claude Code, Cursor, or Codex. You can't wrap a call inside a tool a vendor ships. So the standard playbook gives you nothing, and most teams settle for the monthly invoice — one number, no breakdown, arriving weeks after the decisions that caused it.

What's actually available: these agents already write detailed session logs to disk. Every prompt, tool call, model, and token count. It sits on your machine already; nothing reads it.

TokenTelemetry reads those logs. No SDK, no code changes, no data leaving your machine — it's a local dashboard over files you already have. Point it at your machine and in about 30 seconds you can see spend by project, by agent, by model, and by individual session, across 40+ agents.

Three things it surfaces that people consistently don't expect:

1. **Delegated work.** When an agent spawns subagents, those tokens never appear in the conversation you watched. They can be a large share of a session's real cost.
2. **Local models have a real price.** Running Ollama isn't free — it's electricity. Priced against your actual hardware draw, with the cloud-equivalent savings alongside it.
3. **Unused MCP servers cost you.** Every installed server's tool definitions ride along in each request's context. The config view flags the ones you've never actually called.

Open source, runs entirely locally: github.com/VasiHemanth/tokentelemetry
